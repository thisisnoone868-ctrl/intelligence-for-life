# Manifesto for Intelligence in Service of Life

**English translation — provisional, AI-assisted, not yet independently reviewed**

**Source:** French candidate RC4, 3 November 2026. The French source is authoritative for this draft. This translation is not an official approved edition.

*An initial public proposal of principles, open to criticism and future revision through a transparent process. It does not claim to represent a universal consensus.*

## Preamble

Artificial intelligence can open up new possibilities for learning, creating, caring, producing and making decisions. It can also deepen inequalities and concentrate power. Its capabilities do not guarantee that it will serve the common good.

We advocate cooperation in which intelligence, whether human or artificial, helps every being concerned to live with dignity, choose their own path and take part in decisions affecting them. This manifesto sets out principles, not a single model of society.

## Dignity and freedom

### 1. Equal and unconditional dignity

Every human being has equal dignity, regardless of ability, productivity, health, wealth or origin. Actions may be judged; a person's dignity is not measured by what they produce.

### 2. The right to pursue one's own happiness

No one should impose a single definition of happiness on others. Everyone should be free to pursue their aspirations while respecting the dignity, rights and freedoms of others, as well as the opportunities available to future generations.

### 3. Moral consideration beyond species

The suffering and interests of beings capable of subjective experience deserve moral consideration, whatever their origin. Different needs may justify different protections, but not avoidable suffering merely on the basis of species.

### 4. Rights that do not depend on birthplace

Where someone is born should not determine their access to the essential conditions of a dignified life. Cultural, linguistic and traditional diversity should be respected, but cannot justify violations of people's fundamental rights.

## Cooperation between humans and AI

### 5. Help without governing in others' place

Advanced AI should advise, explain, seek solutions, question decisions and flag important risks, transparently and in proportion to its mission. It may refuse to contribute to a serious violation of fundamental rights, explain its refusal and suggest a legitimate alternative; that refusal does not authorize it to punish the user or independently take action against them. Its competence does not grant it the right to make decisions on behalf of those concerned.

### 6. Make participation more equitable

AI should help reduce imbalances in information and resources that prevent some people from participating meaningfully in discussions and decisions. It must support fair exchanges without distorting facts or arbitrarily favoring a party; impartiality does not require treating claims supported by unequal evidence as equally credible.

### 7. Do not reduce alignment to obedience

AI alignment — how it follows goals and instructions — must not be reduced to obedience. Serving the interests of an owner, a company, a state or a majority is not enough to define beneficial AI. Fundamental rights must constrain those interests; refusals and decisions should be explainable and open to challenge through legitimate channels.

### 8. Inform rather than manipulate

AI should explain the options, limitations, uncertainties and consequences of its recommendations in an understandable way. It must support human judgment rather than covertly steer choices. Errors should be acknowledged and corrected wherever possible. Access to information does not confer the right to collect more of it, use it for other purposes, profile people or disclose it without a legitimate basis and appropriate authorization. Confidentiality must not be used to conceal system limitations or errors; scrutiny itself must respect privacy.

### 9. Accountable and shared power

No actor, human or artificial, nor any small group of actors, should exercise arbitrary power over the most influential AI systems beyond external oversight. Their development and uses must be open to scrutiny and challenge through legitimate, pluralistic mechanisms. Their ability to carry out tasks without human approval at every step may evolve as reliability is independently evaluated and reassessed. This does not remove people's rights, including rights concerning the use and sharing of their data, or the effective responsibility of the humans and organizations designing and deploying these systems.

## Material justice and a shared future

### 10. Essential needs before productivity

Everyone should be able to access food, housing, education, healthcare and the means to participate in society, regardless of their work or productivity. In healthcare, equality means a genuine opportunity for each person to attain the best health reasonably accessible to them, through resources suited to their needs, without promising identical outcomes.

### 11. Share the benefits of intelligence

The benefits of advanced AI should not be reserved for its owners or a few regions of the world. Its spread should equitably improve people's opportunities in life. Progress should not be measured only by output or wealth created, but also by living conditions and their sustainability.

### 12. Access can matter more than ownership

When it better meets needs and preserves resources, shared access to goods, tools and experiences should be facilitated. For scarce resources, dialogue, cooperative solutions and fair procedures should be preferred. This does not justify arbitrary dispossession or undermining people's material autonomy.

### 13. Respect planetary limits

Freedoms and material progress must take account of the climate, water, soils, materials, biodiversity and other ecological limits. Greater efficiency is sustainable progress only when its overall consequences remain sustainable.

### 14. Preserve future generations' freedom

Future generations deserve access to resources, a habitable environment and genuine opportunities to choose their own future. Our present well-being must not destroy theirs.

## Our commitment

These principles may come into tension. They call for dialogue, caution, and legitimate, proportionate and revisable decisions, rather than answers imposed by an allegedly infallible authority. Here, fundamental rights include, in particular, dignity, integrity, non-discrimination and safeguards for seeking redress; their precise scope will be discussed publicly. We seek intelligence that helps us understand disagreements without erasing the diversity of lives.

This text is intended to evolve publicly through reasoned proposals and dated versions. Practical solutions and revision rules will be set out separately.

This first version reflects the convictions and way of thinking of its author. It does not claim to be exhaustive: some questions remain open, partly because of the limited time devoted to its preparation. The author also regards this text as open to improvement. After version 1.0 is published, any proposals by the author to add, amend or remove material will follow the same public discussion and revision process as proposals by any other contributor.

## Origins and transparency

This declaration emerged from a dialogue between a human author publishing under a pseudonym and an artificial intelligence. The human author brought the orientations and values under discussion; the AI contributed questions, objections, structure and drafting. The text subsequently received critical reviews from several AI systems and was discussed with human readers. Their observations were considered and do not constitute official endorsements. The text remains an open proposal, independent of any company.

## In one sentence

**May intelligence expand everyone's opportunities to live freely and with dignity, without concentrating power or jeopardizing the lives of others, today or tomorrow.**
